1. Outbound: `PeerManagerImpl::SendMessages` calls `PushNodeVersion` when `!node.IsInboundConn() && !peer.m_outbound_version_message_sent` (after `MaybeDiscourageAndDisconnect`). Inbound: inside the `NetMsgType::VERSION` handler in `ProcessMessage`, after the peer's `version` was accepted ("Inbound peers send us their version message when they connect").
2. In the `VERSION` handler: a duplicate `version` (already received); the peer lacks the expected service flags for this connection type (`ExpectServicesFromConn() && !HasAllDesirableServiceFlags`); `nVersion < MIN_PEER_PROTO_VERSION` (obsolete); an inbound connection whose nonce matches one of ours (connected to ourselves, `CheckIncomingNonce`). Each sets `fDisconnect = true`.
3. `WTXIDRELAY`, `SENDADDRV2`, `SENDTXRCNCL` and (master) `FEATURE` after verack → `fDisconnect = true` with a `LogDebug`, **no** `Misbehaving`. Rationale: a protocol-ordering mistake is a bug or incompatibility, not evidence of an attack worth discouraging the address for; disconnecting is enough and cheap. `Misbehaving` is used for things like invalid headers/blocks, oversize messages, or invalid compact blocks.
4. `m_misbehavior_mutex` (`bool m_should_discourage GUARDED_BY(m_misbehavior_mutex)` in `struct Peer`). `MaybeDiscourageAndDisconnect` reads and clears it: `NoBan` peers and manual connections are neither disconnected nor discouraged (a warning is logged); local addresses (and inbound onion) are disconnected but not discouraged, since that would hit everyone behind the same address; otherwise `BanMan::Discourage(addr)` + `DisconnectNode(addr)`.
5. `SelectNodeToEvict` in `src/node/eviction.cpp`. It protects (removes from the candidate list) peers from distinct network groups (`CompareNetGroupKeyed`, 4), lowest ping (`ReverseCompareNodeMinPingTime`, 8), peers that recently sent us txs (`CompareNodeTXTime`, 4) and blocks, block-relay-only peers that sent blocks, and a share of the longest-connected, with ratio protection for Tor/I2P/CJDNS/localhost peers (`ProtectEvictionCandidatesByRatio`).
6. Inbound peers in the same network group share one Poisson timer, so a spy that opens many inbound connections (likely from few groups) learns nothing more from extra connections: all of them get the announcement at the same random moment. Per-peer timers would let the spy average many samples to find the first announcer.
7. `AddrInfo::GetNewBucket(nKey, src, netgroupman)` hashes the secret key with the address's group and the **source's** group; `AddrInfo::GetTriedBucket(nKey, netgroupman)` hashes the key with the address and its group. The keyed hash means an attacker cannot predict buckets.
8. During PRESYNC it keeps only a running count of work and a small salted commitment per stretch of headers (1-bit commitments every `HEADER_COMMITMENT_PERIOD` headers), not the headers themselves. Memory per peer stays tiny until the chain proves enough work, then REDOWNLOAD checks the commitments.
9. `MAX_LOCATOR_SZ = 101` in `src/net_processing.cpp`; `getheaders`/`getblocks` with a larger locator → `LogDebug` + `fDisconnect = true`.
